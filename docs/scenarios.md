# Scenarios

This document outlines conceptual scenarios for comparing AI-only acceleration and AW-guided intelligence.

## Scenario 1: AI-only Acceleration

AI capability increases rapidly while existing civilization values remain unchanged.

Expected tendencies:

- faster automation,
- faster extraction,
- stronger competition,
- higher dependency,
- reduced human oversight,
- increased energy and resource demand,
- increased social fragmentation,
- faster systemic fragility.

## Scenario 2: AI with Conventional Safety Only

AI is constrained by conventional safety rules, but not by a natural-law-based wisdom framework.

Expected tendencies:

- reduction of obvious harms,
- better content safety,
- partial risk mitigation,
- but continued alignment with dominant economic and institutional incentives.

This may reduce immediate harms but may not solve civilization-level unsustainability.

## Scenario 3: AW-guided AI

AI capability is evaluated through the Six Principles and additional AW criteria.

Expected tendencies:

- slower destructive optimization,
- stronger oversight,
- greater reversibility,
- clearer accountability,
- more ecological consideration,
- better long-term stability,
- increased regenerative design potential.

## Scenario 4: Performative AW

AW language is adopted symbolically, but incentives and systems remain unchanged.

Expected tendencies:

- superficial ethical language,
- unchanged extraction logic,
- possible greenwashing or wisdom-washing,
- little structural improvement.

This scenario shows why AW must be operational, not merely rhetorical.

## Scenario 5: Integrated AW Civilization OS

AW is integrated into AI systems, governance, engineering, education, ecological design, and social evaluation.

Expected tendencies:

- intelligence becomes connected to regeneration,
- automation remains supervised,
- resource cycles become visible,
- long-term stability becomes an explicit design goal,
- civilization begins shifting from extraction to circulation.
