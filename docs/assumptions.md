# Assumptions

This repository uses transparent assumptions so that the model can be criticized, improved, or replaced.

## A1. AI Reflects Human Value Systems

AI systems are trained, deployed, funded, and evaluated within human institutions.

Therefore, without a higher evaluation layer, AI tends to reflect and amplify dominant human incentives.

## A2. Current Civilization Prioritizes Short-Term Optimization

The current civilization paradigm often prioritizes:

- short-term profit,
- economic growth,
- extraction,
- competition,
- speed,
- convenience,
- automation,
- and scale.

## A3. Intelligence Does Not Automatically Become Wisdom

Higher capability does not automatically create:

- restraint,
- reversibility,
- accountability,
- ecological consistency,
- or long-term stability.

## A4. Automation Can Reduce Oversight

As systems become faster and more automated, human oversight may weaken unless deliberately protected.

## A5. Ecological Limits Are Real

Resource use, energy demand, waste, land use, water use, and ecological disruption cannot be ignored indefinitely.

## A6. AW Acts as a Corrective Evaluation Layer

Artificial Wisdom is assumed to add explicit evaluation through:

- Natural Law,
- Harmony,
- Circulation,
- Structure,
- Order,
- Wa,
- reversibility,
- accountability,
- regenerative capacity.

## A7. AW Is Not Perfect

AW can fail if:

- principles are vague,
- implementation is performative,
- institutions ignore it,
- incentives overpower it,
- or it becomes symbolic rather than operational.
